Paper I strategy — 100 Qs in 120 min

  1. Pass 1 (≈ 55 min): answer every question you are sure of and mark it on the OMR straight away. Put a small dot in the booklet next to any question you're unsure about. Don't stop on any single question.
  2. Pass 2 (≈ 40 min): go back to the dotted questions and use the elimination tricks.
  3. Final 15 min: fill a bubble for EVERY remaining question. There's no negative marking, so a blank can never score but a guess sometimes will. Check the booklet series and roll number on the OMR.

Paper II strategy — you need 66 / 200

  1. That's about 27 correct out of 80 (at 2.5 marks each). Comfortably doable.
  2. Do comprehension first. In 2023 it was questions 1–28, which alone are worth 70 marks. Then do the interpersonal and decision-making questions, then easy reasoning (directions, blood relations, syllogisms), and finally maths.
  3. Skip lengthy calculations. Fill every bubble at the end.

Eat, drink water and don't discuss Paper I answers with anyone. Skim the CSAT formula box once.

What Paper I actually looks like

There have been only two MCS Prelims under the current two-paper pattern: 4 Aug 2018 (2017 advertisement) and 4 Nov 2023. Tomorrow's is the third. So “the last 5 exams” don't exist. This analysis uses those two papers plus the 2022 Meghalaya Police Service (MPS) Prelims, which the same Commission set.

What this means for tomorrow

  • Economy is the single biggest block. The questions are concept-based (CRR, core inflation, deficits, national income, balance of payments) and don't depend on the news, so revising them gives reliable marks.
  • Geography is mostly physical geography at NCERT level: rocks, seismic waves, pressure belts, ocean currents, albedo.
  • Meghalaya was only 2% in 2023, but the 2025 syllabus now names it as a separate item, so expect more questions. This is an inference, because no paper under the new syllabus exists yet.
  • Current affairs covers roughly the 12–18 months before the exam. Most are defence exercises, summits, reports and indices, sports and species in the news.
  • Difficulty is easy to moderate. Nearly everything is NCERT or newspaper level.
  • The official key can be wrong. In 2023, MPSC corrected 3 answers (Q58, Q74, Q76) after review, and some questions were debatable, for example Q72 which gave “Karl Marx” as “Father of Demographic Studies”. Pick the answer the setter most likely intended.

Tricks for each question type

Techniques that work on MPSC's style of questions.

1 · Statement questions (“which is/are correct”)

  • Judge one statement at a time, then strike out option codes. If you are sure statement i is wrong, every option containing i is gone. Often one sure call leaves just one or two options.
  • Look for the planted wrong fact. MPSC often slips in one wrong detail: a year (Plassey “1750” instead of 1757), a person (Lucknow Pact “Nehru–Jinnah”), or a technology (Covishield as “mRNA”).
  • Be suspicious of extreme words such as only, all, never, always, completely. For example, “Centre–state sharing is 50:50 for all states” was false; the NE and Himalayan states get 90:10.
  • Hedged statements such as may, can, generally, some are usually true.
2023 Q59 · official key C
Plassey: i. Siraj-ud-Daulah vs EIC under Clive · ii. fought on 23 June 1750 · iii. Mir Jafar betrayed · iv. marked the beginning of British rule.
→ Statement ii has the wrong year (it was 1757), so drop every option containing ii. Only (c) i, iii, iv is left.

2 · “Which is NOT correct” and double negatives

In 2023, Q8 (sports not featured) and Q66 (which is not correct) both used this style. Circle the word NOT in your booklet before you read the options. A common mistake is picking the true statement when the question asks for the false one.

3 · “How many pairs are correctly matched?”

With this format you can't eliminate options, so you have to judge every pair. Mark each pair ✓ or ✗ in the margin and count them at the end.

2023 Q25 · official key C (two)
Cyclone name → country that proposed it: Asani–Sri Lanka ✓ · Tej–Bangladesh ✗ (India) · Biparjoy–India ✗ (Bangladesh) · Asna–Pakistan ✓ → two. Cyclone names come from a list the 13 member countries propose in turn, so the name tells you who proposed it, not where the cyclone hits.

4 · Sequence and arrangement questions

Work out only the first and last items. That usually leaves one option, and you can skip the middle.

2023 Q11 · official key D
Arrange by density, highest first: Hydrogen, Oxygen, Carbon, Nitrogen → Carbon is a solid so it is densest, and hydrogen is lightest. Only (d) iii, ii, iv, i starts with carbon and ends with hydrogen.

5 · Identify from clues

Find the most unique clue and ignore the rest. “Longest river bridge” plus latitude 26°–29°N and longitude 91°–97°E point to Arunachal (the Dhola–Sadiya bridge is at the Assam–Arunachal border). The longitude range alone rules out HP and Uttarakhand.

6 · Guessing when you really don't know

  • With no negative marking, never leave a question blank.
  • In “select using codes” questions, the option that includes most statements or all of the above was right in several 2023 questions (Q5, Q21, Q28, Q35, Q36, Q49, Q99). Use it only as a last-resort tie-breaker, never as your first thought.
  • If two options are near-opposites, the answer is usually one of them.
  • Your first instinct on a fact you studied is usually right. Change an answer only when you remember something specific.

2 · Sixth Schedule and traditional institutions

  • Sixth Schedule (Art 244(2) and 275(1)) covers tribal areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram. A classic trap is to offer Manipur, Nagaland or Arunachal as options.
  • Meghalaya has 3 Autonomous District Councils: Khasi Hills ADC (KHADC), Jaintia Hills ADC (JHADC) and Garo Hills ADC (GHADC). The Khasi and Garo councils date from 1952.
  • ADCs can make laws on land, forests (not reserved forests), shifting cultivation, village administration, inheritance, marriage and social customs. These laws need the Governor's assent.
  • Khasi: Hima = traditional state, headed by the Syiem. Some himas have a Lyngdoh, Sirdar or Doloi instead. Dorbar Shnong = village council, headed by the Rangbah Shnong.
  • Jaintia (Pnar): the Doloi heads an Elaka.
  • Garo (A·chik): the Nokma is the village chief and custodian of clan land (A·king).

4 · Environment, caves and protected areas

  • Nokrek Biosphere Reserve (Garo Hills) joined UNESCO's Man and Biosphere network in 2009. It is home to the wild Citrus indica (memang narang).
  • National parks: Balpakram (South Garo Hills, “land of perpetual winds”) and Nokrek.
  • Wildlife sanctuaries: Nongkhyllem (Ri-Bhoi), Siju (South Garo Hills), Baghmara Pitcher Plant Sanctuary, Narpuh (Jaintia Hills).
  • Mawphlang Sacred Grove (Law Kyntang). Sacred groves are protected by customary law.
  • Caves: Krem Liat Prah is the longest natural cave in India (Jaintia Hills). Krem Puri is the longest sandstone cave in the world. Siju (“cave of bats”) is on the Simsang river. Mawmluh (near Sohra) is where the stalagmite came from that defines the Meghalayan Age, the latest stage of the Holocene, which began about 4,200 years ago (ICS, 2018).
  • Living root bridges (jingkieng jri) are made from Ficus elastica roots. They were added to UNESCO's tentative list in 2022. The double-decker bridge is at Nongriat. The 2023 key says the longest is at Mawkyrnot.
  • State symbols: animal Clouded leopard · bird Hill myna · flower Lady's slipper orchid · tree Gamhar (Gmelina arborea).
  • The NGT banned rat-hole coal mining in 2014. Rat-hole mines cause acid mine drainage, which is why the Kopili and Lukha rivers turned acidic.
  • Byrnihat (Ri-Bhoi, on the Assam border) was ranked the most polluted city in the world in IQAir's World Air Quality Report 2024. check for newer data

Economy 20% of 2023

Economy questions test concepts, so if you understand the logic of each concept you can answer the question however it is worded.

Money and banking

  • Demand deposits = savings + current accounts (withdrawable on demand). Time deposits = fixed deposits. 2023
  • Bank money is created through credit creation, when banks lend out deposits. 2023
  • CRR (cash kept with the RBI) and SLR (liquid assets kept by the bank itself) are direct instruments. Repo, reverse repo, OMO and MSF are indirect instruments. MPS 2022
  • Logic to remember: a lower CRR, repo rate or SLR means more money with banks, so money supply ↑. A higher rate means money supply ↓. 2023
  • OMO means the RBI buys or sells government securities. 2018
  • The Basel II pillars are minimum capital, supervisory review and market discipline. “Exchange control” is NOT a pillar. 2023
  • MICR (Magnetic Ink Character Recognition) is a 9-digit code on cheques. IFSC is 11 characters. 2023
  • FEMA 1999 replaced FERA 1973. 2023 SDRs are issued by the IMF. 2018

Inflation

  • Core inflation is headline inflation excluding volatile items (food and fuel). 2023 — the key was corrected to this
  • The RBI targets CPI inflation of 4% ± 2% (flexible inflation targeting since 2016).
  • Terms: stagflation = inflation with stagnation · disinflation = inflation slowing · deflation = falling prices · shrinkflation = same price but a smaller pack MPS 2022
  • To fight inflation: raise interest rates, raise taxes, cut government spending. Doing the opposite is inflationary. 2018

Government finance (budget)

TermMeaning
Fiscal deficitTotal expenditure − (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts) = the borrowing needed 2023
Revenue deficitRevenue expenditure − revenue receipts

Vote on account vs Interim budgetA vote on account covers expenditure only. An interim budget covers both expenditure and receipts. 2023

National income

  • GNP = GDP + net factor income from abroad · NNP = GNP − depreciation 2023 · National income = NNP at factor cost = NNP(MP) − indirect taxes + subsidies.
  • The circular flow includes goods and services, factor payments and spending. Depreciation is not part of the flow. 2023
  • Balance of payments: the current account covers goods, services, income and transfers. The capital account covers FDI, FPI and loans. Imports are debits, so importing machinery is a debit in the current account (it is trade in goods). 2023
  • A closed economy has no exports or imports. 2023

Geography 16% of 2023

Mostly Class 11 NCERT physical geography, plus India's borders and rivers.

Earth's interior and rocks

  • P waves (primary) are the fastest and arrive first on a seismogram. They travel through solids and liquids. 2023 S waves can't pass through liquids, which is how we know the outer core is liquid. Surface waves (Love, Rayleigh) arrive last and do the most damage.
  • Igneous rocks form from magma: basalt 2023 and granite. Sedimentary: sandstone, limestone, coal. Metamorphic: limestone → marble, shale → slate, sandstone → quartzite, granite → gneiss.
  • Continental rise is between the continental slope and the abyssal plain, made of sediment about 4–5 km deep. 2023

Atmosphere and climate

  • Pressure belts: equatorial low = doldrums (ITCZ) 2023 · subtropical high at ~30° = horse latitudes · subpolar low at ~60° · polar high.
  • Winds: trade winds blow from the subtropical high to the equator. Westerlies give the roaring forties, furious fifties and shrieking sixties. Trade winds are driven by convection. 2018
  • Coriolis force is zero at the equator and strongest at the poles. Cyclones rotate anticlockwise in the northern hemisphere.
  • Hail forms by freezing in cumulonimbus clouds. 2023 key
  • Albedo: fresh snow is highest, then clouds and sand, and water and forest are lowest. 2023
  • Regional names: hurricane (Atlantic), typhoon (NW Pacific), tropical cyclone (Indian Ocean and S Pacific), willy-willy (Australia).
  • China-type climate = warm, moist summer and cool, dry winter, with summer typhoons. 2023 Mediterranean climate is the opposite: winter rain.
  • Tamil Nadu gets most of its rain from the NE (retreating) monsoon, and J&K from western disturbances. 2018

Common-sense answers, no maths

The order to attempt Paper II

  1. Comprehension (Q1–28 in 2023). Read the questions before the passage. Answer only from the passage, not from what you already know.
  2. Interpersonal and decision-making questions, using the rules below.
  3. Quick reasoning: directions, blood relations, analogies, syllogisms, coding.
  4. Maths and data interpretation, choosing only the short ones.
  5. Fill every remaining bubble.

Comprehension tricks

  • Options using extreme words (always, only, never, all) are usually wrong in inference questions.
  • “Main idea” questions: pick the option that covers the whole passage, not a single paragraph.
  • For vocabulary questions, replace the word with each option in its sentence. For example, “congregate” = assemble.
  • “Assumption” means something the author takes for granted without stating it. “Inference” means something that must follow from what is written.

Interpersonal and decision-making: the answer the Commission wants

Pick the option that is:calm, respectful and private (not a public confrontation) · follows rules and the law · solves the actual problem · informs the proper authority when safety or legality is involved · neither avoids the problem nor overreacts.
  • “Your head scolds you in front of everyone for a colleague's error.” → Stay calm now and explain the facts to the head privately later. Don't blame the colleague publicly, and don't simply accept the blame without saying anything.
  • “A suspicious bag near you.” → Don't touch it. Move people away and inform the police or security.
  • “Pressured to win a case using money.” → Refuse, act lawfully, and report the pressure if needed.
  • “Someone hits your car at a signal.” → Check whether anyone is hurt, stay calm, exchange details or follow legal procedure. Don't pick a fight.

Probability (A or B)P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B). Two people contradict each other with probability p₁(1−p₂) + (1−p₁)p₂
Speed, time, workD = S × T. Working together: 1/T = 1/a + 1/b

Reasoning shortcuts

  • Directions: always draw it. North is up. Turning left while facing south means you now face east.
  • Blood relations: draw a family tree with + for male, − for female, = for married couples and a vertical line for generations.
  • Syllogisms: draw Venn circles. “All A are B” + “No B is C” means No A is C. When unsure, “Some…” conclusions follow only if they hold in every possible drawing.
  • Data sufficiency: test statement I alone, then II alone, then both together. Don't solve fully; stop once you know an answer can be found.

Worked PYQs

2023 Q38 · Car sold at 18% loss; +₹99,000 would have given 15% profit. Price for 10% profit? The gap between a 15% profit and an 18% loss is 33% of CP. So 0.33·CP = 99,000, which gives CP = ₹3,00,000. For a 10% profit, sell at ₹3,30,000 (b).
2023 Q39 · Fewest complete years for money to more than triple at 40% CI? 1.4² = 1.96, 1.4³ = 2.744 (not yet 3), 1.4⁴ = 3.84 → 4 years (b).
2023 Q41 · 40 L of milk; 4 L replaced by water three times. How much milk is left? 40 × (1 − 4/40)³ = 40 × 0.729 = 29.16 L.
2023 Q43 · A tap of diameter d empties a tank in 40 min. A tap of diameter 2d? The area is 4 times as big, so the flow is 4 times faster: 40/4 = 10 min.
2023 Q31 · When between 4 and 5 o'clock are the hands at right angles? |120 − 5.5M| = 90 gives M = 30/5.5 = 5 5⁄11 min past 4, or M = 210/5.5 = 38 2⁄11 min past 4.
2023 Q30 · Numbers from 1–45 divisible by 3, in ascending order: which is 9th? The list is 3, 6, 9, … so the 9th is 3 × 9 = 27 (d).
2023 Q76 · 50 m south, turn left and go 20 m, turn north and go 30 m, then walk home. Which direction? Start at (0,0). Go to (0,−50), then (20,−50) (facing south, left is east), then (20,−20). Home is up and to the left, so you walk North-West.
2023 Q79 · A and B are sisters; A is D's mother; B's daughter C is married to F; G is A's husband. How is B related to F? F is married to B's daughter, so F is B's son-in-law and B is F's mother-in-law. The details about A, D and G are distractors.
2018 Q30 · 15% discount, 19% gain wanted. How far above cost should the price be marked? MP/CP = 119/85 = 1.4, so mark it 40% above cost.
2018 Q31 · A tells the truth 60% of the time, B 70%. How often do they contradict each other? 0.6 × 0.3 + 0.4 × 0.7 = 0.18 + 0.28 = 46%.
2018 Q37 · Angle between the hands at 10:30? |30×10 − 5.5×30| = |300 − 165| = 135°.
2018 Q39 · How many 4-digit numbers can be made from 0, 1, 2, 3? With repetition: 3 × 4 × 4 × 4 = 192 (0 can't be the first digit). Without repetition: 3 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 18.
2018 Q40 · Probability of picking a multiple of 4 or 7 from 1–25? Multiples of 4: 6 numbers. Multiples of 7: 3 numbers. None are both (28 is above 25). So 9/25.
