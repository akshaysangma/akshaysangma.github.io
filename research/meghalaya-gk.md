## 1. Formation and constitutional basis

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Constitutional enabler | **22nd Amendment Act, 1969** inserted **Art. 244A**, which let Parliament create an *autonomous State within Assam* (assent 25 Sep 1969) |
| Autonomous State | **Assam Reorganisation (Meghalaya) Act, 1969** → autonomous state inside Assam from **2 April 1970** |
| Full statehood | **North-Eastern Areas (Reorganisation) Act, 1971** → full state on **21 January 1972** (Meghalaya Day). Manipur and Tripura also became states on 21 Jan 1972 |
| Carved from | Two Assam districts: United Khasi-Jaintia Hills and Garo Hills |
| Name | "Meghalaya" = *abode of clouds*. Coined by geographer **Shiba Prasad (S.P.) Chatterjee** |
| Capital | Shillong ("Scotland of the East" in British times) |
| First CM | **Williamson A. Sangma** (2 Apr 1970 – 3 Mar 1978; CM of the autonomous state first, then of the full state) |
| First Governor | **B.K. (Braj Kumar) Nehru**. He was also Governor of Assam, and held Meghalaya from 21 Jan 1972 to 19 Sep 1973 |
| High Court | **High Court of Meghalaya** set up on **23 March 2013** under the North-Eastern Areas (Reorganisation) and Other Related Laws (Amendment) Act, 2012. Manipur and Tripura HCs were created by the same law; before this, Meghalaya was under the Gauhati HC |
| FLAG | One source calls Meghalaya the "21st state". NE state-numbering is not consistent across sources, so don't memorise an ordinal |

## 3. Geography

### 3.1 Basics
| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Area | 22,429 km² |
| Borders | Assam to the north and east; **Bangladesh** to the south and west |
| Plateau | **Meghalaya (Shillong) Plateau**, a NE extension of the Peninsular plateau. It is separated from the Chota Nagpur plateau by the **Garo–Rajmahal gap (Malda gap)**, a down-faulted zone filled with Ganga–Brahmaputra alluvium |
| Hill regions (west → east) | Garo Hills, Khasi Hills, Jaintia Hills |

### 3.2 Official languages (changed in 2026)
- Old rule: **Meghalaya State Language Act, 2005**. English was the official language; Khasi and Garo were *associate* official languages.
- **16 Apr 2026**: Cabinet approved the change. **27 Apr 2026**: Governor promulgated the **Meghalaya Official Languages Ordinance, 2026** (Ordinance No. 1 of 2026) under **Art. 213(1)**.
- Effect: **English, Khasi and Garo** are all official languages, from a date the government will notify. The 2005 Act is repealed.
  - English stays the language of the civil and criminal courts.
  - Assembly business continues under the Meghalaya State Legislature (Continuance of the English Language) Act, 1980.
- **Aug 2026**: Cabinet approved the **Meghalaya Official Languages Bill, 2026** to replace the Ordinance.
  - FLAG: I could not confirm whether the Assembly passed it in the Aug–Sep 2026 autumn session (as of 27 Sep 2026).
- Related: in **July 2018** the Assembly unanimously passed a resolution asking for Khasi and Garo to be added to the **8th Schedule**. They are not in it yet (as of Sep 2026).

### 3.5 Caves (Meghalaya is India's main caving region)
- **Krem Liat Prah** (East Jaintia Hills) is the **longest cave in India / South Asia**.
  - FLAG on length: Wikipedia says 30,957 m; newer reports say about 34 km, and the figure keeps growing as passages are linked.
  - Its large chamber is called the "Aircraft Hangar".
- **Krem Puri** (Laitsohum, near Mawsynram, East Khasi Hills) is the **world's longest sandstone cave**: **24,583 m**, surveyed in 2016. Mosasaur fossils have been found in it. Local name means "fairy cave".
- **Mawmluh Cave** (near Sohra, ~7.2 km long) holds the **stalagmite that defines the start of the Meghalayan Age**.
  - The Meghalayan is the latest age of the **Holocene**. The sequence is Greenlandian → Northgrippian → **Meghalayan**, and the Meghalayan began **4,200 years ago** (4,250 years before 2000 CE).
  - FLAG on the ratification date: the IUGS Executive Committee ratified it on **14 June 2018**, but the public announcement was in **July 2018**. Sources quote either date.
  - In Oct 2022 IUGS listed Mawmluh among the "First 100 IUGS Geological Heritage Sites".
- Other long caves (Wikipedia): Krem Kotsati-Umlawan 21.5 km, Krem Tyngheng-Diengjem 21.25 km, Krem Umthloo-Synrang Labbit 18.2 km (all Jaintia Hills).
- Exploration: the **Meghalaya Adventurers Association** has led systematic surveys since the 1990s. As of 2015, about 1,580 cave sites were identified and ~427 km surveyed. Siju Cave is in South Garo Hills.

### 3.6 Living root bridges (Jingkieng Jri)
- Built by Khasi and Jaintia communities by training the aerial roots of ***Ficus elastica*** (India rubber tree) across streams. A usable bridge takes about 10–15 years to grow.
- Found in 70+ villages. Best known: the **double-decker bridge at Nongriat** (East Khasi Hills). Others at Rewai and Mawlynnong.
- **UNESCO:** added to the **Tentative List in 2022** as "Jingkieng Jri: Living Root Bridge Cultural Landscapes".
- **30 Jan 2026:** India submitted the full nomination, "Jingkieng Jri / Lyu Chrai Cultural Landscape", for the **2026–27** evaluation cycle. Decision pending (as of Sep 2026).
- UNESCO's tentative list also has a separate "Meghalayan Age Caves" entry.

## 4. People and culture

### 4.1 Communities
- **Khasi** (largest group), **Jaintia / Pnar** (the Jaintia sub-group of the wider Khasi family) and **Garo** (A·chik).
  - Khasi and Pnar are **Austroasiatic (Mon-Khmer)** languages; Garo is **Tibeto-Burman (Bodo-Garo)**.
- Share of speakers in 2011: Khasi 33.8%, Garo 31.6%, Pnar 10.7%, Bengali 6.4%.
- Smaller groups: Hajong, Koch, Rabha, Biate, Boro, Rajbongshi, Karbi, Tiwa, Dimasa, Kuki, Nepali.

### 4.2 Matrilineal system
- **Khasi / Jaintia**
  - Children take the **mother's clan name (kur)**.
  - The **youngest daughter, *Ka Khadduh***, inherits the ancestral house ("ancestral hearth") and the bulk of the property, and looks after her parents and unmarried siblings.
  - The husband usually moves into the wife's family home.
  - The maternal uncle has a traditional role in family affairs.
- **Garo**
  - Children belong to the mother's clan (**ma·chong**).
  - The parents choose one daughter, usually the youngest, as heiress: the **Nokna**. Her husband is the **Nokrom**.
  - Sons do not inherit maternal property.
- Exam trap: **matrilineal ≠ matriarchal**. Political and clan authority traditionally lies with men (e.g. maternal uncles, the Syiem). Only descent and inheritance go through the mother.

### 6.3 Sixth Schedule and Autonomous District Councils (ADCs)
- Legal basis: **Art. 244(2)** and **Art. 275(1)**. The Sixth Schedule covers tribal areas of **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram**.
- ADCs can make laws on land (other than reserved forest), forests, jhum (shifting cultivation), village administration, inheritance, marriage and social customs. They also run village courts and collect certain taxes and royalties.

### 7.2 Agriculture and horticulture
- Mostly agrarian; **shifting cultivation (jhum)** is still practised, especially in Garo Hills.
- Main crops: rice, maize, potato, ginger, **turmeric**, pineapple, **Khasi mandarin**, banana, black pepper, areca nut, strawberry, tea and coffee (small).
- **Lakadong turmeric** (West Jaintia Hills) has high curcumin: **6.5–7.5%** (ICAR testing) against 2–3% in common varieties. **Mission Lakadong (2018–2023)** aimed to raise output from 20,000 to 50,000 MT a year.
- Honey, Sohra (Cherrapunji) produce and floriculture (orchids, anthurium) are growth areas.

## 8. Firsts, superlatives and current affairs (quick list)
- **First state in India to adopt its own State Water Policy**: Meghalaya State Water Policy, 2019, approved by Cabinet on **12 July 2019**.
- **First reservoir hydel project in NE India**: Umiam Stage-I (1965).
- **Longest cave in South Asia**: Krem Liat Prah. **Longest sandstone cave in the world**: Krem Puri.
- **Youngest geological age**: named after Meghalaya (Meghalayan, 2018).
- **Wettest places**: Mawsynram (average annual) and Sohra (month and 12-month records).
- **Tallest plunge waterfall in India**: Nohkalikai (340 m).
- **World's most polluted metropolitan area (IQAir report for 2024)**: **Byrnihat**, an industrial town on the Assam–Meghalaya border in Ri-Bhoi, with annual PM2.5 of 128.2 µg/m³. The state government disputed the finding.
- **39th National Games**: Meghalaya will host them in **2027** (Feb–Mar 2027 per the IOA; first time for the state). Venues: Shillong (JN Stadium), Tura (P.A. Sangma Stadium), Jowai.
- **2026**: Khasi and Garo made official languages (Ordinance, April 2026).
- **2026**: anti-uranium Assembly resolution (26 Aug 2026).
- **2026**: new MCS (civil services) **Paper VI on Meghalaya** covering history, culture, traditional systems, society and economy (Cabinet, 13 Aug 2026).
