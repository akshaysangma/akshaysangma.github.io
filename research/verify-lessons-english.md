# English teaching-content fact-check log

Scope: modules e01 to e06. I checked every lesson body and reference table, all 36 worked examples, every trap, and the explanation hooks in all 180 practice items. The formulas arrays are empty in all six modules. The answer keys were already verified (see verify-english.md).

What was checked: about 200 one-word substitutes, including the root bank and the -logy, -cide, -cracy, phobia, mania and place tables; about 100 confusable sets; about 90 spellings plus the spelling rules; 154 idioms (after removing 2 duplicates) plus the classical-idiom notes; 40 proverbs; 63 similes; 74 phrasal verbs; all grammar, voice, narration and error-spotting tables; and about 75 synonym/antonym rows.

Most entries were confirmed from standard reference knowledge. I fetched dictionary sources (URLs below) for the contested-usage items and for the molehill idiom. Several Oxford Learner's and Cambridge pages returned 403, so Merriam-Webster, British Council and Wikipedia were used instead.

Checked and kept:
- Emigrant/Immigrant (e01 table and trap) vs Emigrate/Immigrate (e02). These are different word classes, the wording is consistent, and each fits its module's question type.
- Farther/further, later/latter and beside/besides appear in both e02 and e04. The wording is consistent, and the e04 comparison and preposition tables need these rows.
- Principal/principle is repeated in e02 lessons 2 and 3. This is deliberate and already labelled as a repeat.
- e06 lesson 1 (the Mawsynram passage) and e06-p17 (a para-jumble on the same content) share a topic but are in different formats. Not changed.
- The 2022 paper items (2022-1 to 2022-50) were compared against all 180 practice items. No practice item repeats a 2022 item. e02-p04 uses the same option family as 2022-11 (ingenious/ingenuous/indigenous) but has a different sentence and a different key.

## Checked and left unchanged (defensible)
- The following are standard exam conventions and were left as they are: "Make both ends meet" (MW lists "make (both) ends meet"), "between the devil and the deep sea", "throw dust in someone's eyes", "end in smoke" and "cousin brother". The last three are Indian English.
- Pronoun order 2-3-1 / 1-2-3, each other/one another (already labelled a traditional exam rule), "the reason is that" and "the taller of the two" are traditional exam rules. No absolute false claim was made.
- The contagious-by-touch vs infectious distinction is the standard exam convention.
- The claim in the e03 "why" (12 of 50 questions on fixed expressions) was verified against items 2022-7, 2022-10 and 2022-16 to 2022-25.
