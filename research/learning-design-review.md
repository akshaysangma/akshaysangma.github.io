Scope: the IPO Meghalaya prep site as implemented in `assets/app.js`, `index.html`, `research/MODULE_SCHEMA.md` and `research/modules/*.json`, read on 2026-09-27.

## 2. Flow critique

### 2.1 Is "diagnostic → phases → mocks" the right order?

- **Prerequisite order: yes.** Arithmetic → algebra/trig → coordinate/3D → calculus/probability follows the maths dependency chain. Slotting English/GK modules between maths modules already spaces the subjects out. This part matches the evidence and Math Academy's "master prerequisites first" idea.
- **Diagnostic: right idea, poorly used.**
  1. Its result changes nothing. A pretest mainly pays off by directing attention (Pan & Carpenter 2023), or through placement (Math Academy's 30–45 min adaptive diagnostic, expertise reversal). The site gets neither.
  2. 150 minutes on unlearned material on day 1 is a big cost for a learner who is weak in maths.
  3. It uses up the only real paper as a benchmark. 166 of its 175 questions are later studied in module "Past-paper Qs" tabs, so the Exam-readiness step "Re-attempt IPO-2022 — compare with your diagnostic" measures memory of those items, not readiness.
  4. Keep the diagnostic, but use it for placement: per-module scores → "skip lessons" or "start at practice" suggestions. Allow a maths-only 75-Q version. Retire the re-attempt comparison.
- **Phases then mocks: the missing piece is cumulative review *during* the phases.** Phase 1 material is only practised again when you get something wrong, or in the final mocks. Checkpoints test only the current phase. Cepeda et al. 2008 show the best review gap scales with the retention interval. If the exam is 2–3 months away, Phase 1 topics need reviews every week or two until the exam, not a queue that deletes items after about 27 days.

### 2.2 "Read long lesson, then practise" vs. the alternatives

| Alternative | Verdict for this learner | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Pretest first** (5 items per module) | **Adopt, lightly.** | Moderate evidence, cheap to build, and it gives a test-out route (expertise reversal). The benefit is mostly item-specific, so choose the 5 items to cover the lesson list. Evidence for pretesting multi-step procedures in *weak* learners is thin, so keep it to 5 items and make it low-stakes. Do not use it to replace instruction. |
| **Short chunks with embedded checks** | **Adopt.** | Combines retrieval practice (strong) and segmenting (moderate). It also turns the "Mark as read" click into an actual retrieval act. |
| **Fading worked examples + self-explanation** | **Adopt.** | Atkinson/Renkl evidence. Solutions are already written one step per line, so a "reveal next step" UI is almost free. |
| **Interleaved mixed practice** | **Adopt after the first blocked pass.** | Strong for maths. Blocked-first is the safer order for a weak learner (row 5). Do not interleave vocabulary lists at first exposure (row 6). |
| **Cumulative review of earlier modules** | **Adopt. This is the biggest gap.** | Spacing and interleaving together. It also fixes the "retire after 16 days" problem. |
| **Productive failure (solve before any instruction)** | **Don't make it the default for maths.** | g = 0.36 for conceptual knowledge and transfer, but this exam rewards fast standard procedures, and the effect depends on well-designed invention tasks. The 5-item pretest captures the cheap part. |

Suggested module flow:

**Pretest (5, answers shown) → [lesson chunk → 2–3 checks] × n → faded worked examples (step reveal + one "why" prompt) → blocked practice L1→L2 → mixed set (≈30% earlier modules) → held-out module check → delayed re-checks at about 3 and 10 days, then folded into the daily cumulative set until the exam.**

### 2.3 What mastery should mean

The current rule (all read + 80% attempted + 70% latest-attempt accuracy) rewards reading and re-answering, and is never re-checked. Proposed:

1. **Unlock** (Khan's "Familiar"): ≥ 70% *first-attempt* accuracy on the module's practice items. `firstAcc` is already computed in `moduleStats()`.
2. **Mastered** (Khan's "Proficient"): ≥ 80% first-attempt accuracy on a **held-out check set** of 8 items the learner has never seen, with at least 3 of them level 2 or 3. If it fails: the site lists the lessons behind the missed items, and the learner retakes on a different held-out subset. This is Bloom's corrective loop and parallel retest.
3. **Retained** (successive relearning): ≥ 70% first attempt on a 5-item re-check about 3 days later, then about 10 days later. Below 60% on any later cumulative or delayed check sets the module to "needs review" (it decays, as on Khan Academy).
4. **Speed** (Phases 3–4 onward, and all arithmetic): median time on level 1–2 items ≤ 70 s, matching the exam budget in `TRACKS.maths.secPerQ`.
5. Lessons read = progress display only, not a gate.

With 20–40 practice items per module, reserving 8 for the check leaves 12–32 for practice. Modules with 20–25 items (m12 has 20) may need about 5 more items written.

## 4. Things in the current design the research says work against learning

1. **Mastery is based on the latest attempt after feedback** (app.js:78–80). Answering again straight after reading the explanation raises "accuracy". That measures immediate performance, which Bjork and Deans for Impact warn about ("completion of assignments and absence of errors does not necessarily equate to long-lasting learning", Deans for Impact, "Common misconceptions" section). It is also never re-checked later.
2. **Reading is rewarded as progress.** "Mark as read" is required for mastery and makes up 40% of the progress bar. Rereading and passive reading are rated low utility (Dunlosky et al. 2013).
3. **Review items are deleted after the 16-day step** (about 27 days after the first miss). With a multi-month preparation, items learned in Phase 1 leave the system well before the exam. This mismatches the spacing-to-retention-interval finding (Cepeda et al. 2008).
4. **Only wrong answers are reviewed.** On a 4-option MCQ, a blind guess is right 25% of the time, and those items are treated as known forever. Low-confidence correct answers benefit from feedback and review (Butler et al. 2008).
5. **Fully blocked practice until the final phase.** Each module practises one chapter's methods, and checkpoints test one phase. Learners never have to *pick* a method until the mocks, which is exactly the skill interleaving trains (Rohrer et al. 2020). Blocked practice also feels more effective than it is (Kornell & Bjork 2008), so the learner's own sense of readiness will be inflated.
6. **Using the one real paper as both diagnostic and final benchmark.** Most of its questions are studied in between, so the "compare with your diagnostic" gain is inflated.
7. **Weaker concern, not strictly counter-productive:** the 150-minute diagnostic on day 1 costs a lot and currently informs nothing. Either use it for placement or shorten it (maths-only, or a 60–75-question sample).

What is *right* and should be kept: worked examples before practice with a "try first" prompt; step-by-step explanations with the distractors built from common mistakes; levelled practice; prerequisite-ordered phases; English/GK spread between maths modules; timed mocks with a question palette, per-section time and blank-answer analysis; the weak-topic mock; the arithmetic speed drill; and weighting maths mocks by past-paper topic frequency.
