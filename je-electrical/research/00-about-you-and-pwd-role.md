**QB.2 What is the biggest problem in power distribution in Garo Hills?**
A: Give the honest, measured answer:
- long rural feeders with hilly terrain, so faults are slow to locate and restore;
- tail-end low voltage;
- trees on lines during the monsoon;
- distribution transformer failures from overloading and poor earthing;
- commercial losses from unmetered or tampered connections.
Then say what is being done: RDSS loss-reduction works and smart meters, network strengthening, and the 220 kV Nangalbibra substation, which improved supply to Garo Hills.
Likely follow-up: "What did you personally do about it?" (use your QA.10 story)

**QB.3 How is a new LT service connection given?**
A: The broad steps:
1. The applicant applies with ID and ownership or occupancy proof.
2. A site inspection checks the load, the nearest pole or DT and the internal wiring, which must have a test report from a licensed contractor.
3. An estimate and demand note are issued, and the applicant pays the charges and security deposit.
4. The service line is drawn and the meter is installed and sealed.
5. The consumer is entered in the billing system.
The Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Rules 2020 set time limits for a new connection. Check the current MePDCL and MSERC Supply Code timelines.
Source: Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Rules 2020. Meghalaya-specific timelines: (unverified)

**QB.4 How do you make sure your linemen work safely?**
A: No work on a line without:
1. a written shutdown or permit-to-work from the authorised person;
2. confirming the line is dead with a tester;
3. discharging it and earthing it on both sides of the work spot;
4. PPE: insulated gloves, helmet, safety belt and shoes;
5. a danger board at the isolating point.
After the work, every man and earth is accounted for before the permit is returned and the line recharged. Most fatal accidents happen when one of these steps is skipped under pressure to restore supply.
Source: CEA (Measures relating to Safety and Electric Supply) Regulations 2023, see the rules deck.

**QB.5 How is working in a utility different from working in PWD?**
A: A utility supplies power and its focus is the network: lines, substations, metering, revenue and losses. PWD is the owner and builder of government buildings, so its focus is the consumer installation inside and around the building: design, estimates, tendering, supervision of contractors, measurement and quality, and maintenance afterwards. In PWD the JE is the department's eyes on site and is accountable through the MB.

## 3. The PWD JE (Electrical) role

**QC.1 How is Meghalaya PWD organised, and where does the electrical wing sit?**
A: The PWD has a Roads wing and a Buildings wing, each under a Chief Engineer. The post I applied for is under PWD (Buildings). Its electrical works are handled by an Electrical Division under an Executive Engineer, with electrical sub-divisions, including one at Tura, under Assistant Executive Engineers and Junior Engineers in the field. The department's minister is Deputy CM Shri Prestone Tynsong, who holds PWD (Roads) and PWD (Buildings).
Source: megpwd.gov.in/org-setup.html; PWD (Building) tender PW/CE/BD/117/2023/28 (2 Mar 2026); portfolio notification CA.04/2023/19 (16 Sep 2025). The sub-division list is from an undated secondary directory (unverified).

**QC.2 Which Schedule of Rates does Meghalaya PWD use for electrical works?**
A: The department's own "MPWD (Buildings) Electrical Division Schedule of Rates 2021-2022 for Electrical Works". Building works use the MPWD (Buildings) SOR 2021-22, and tenders are on the M.P.W.D. F-2 form of agreement. Contractors quote at par, above or below the SOR, and need a valid electrical licence. Where an item is not in the SOR, the rate is worked out by rate analysis from market rates of material and labour, plus the contractor's overheads and profit.
Source: tender PW/CE/BD/117/2023/28, Chief Engineer PWD (Building), 2 Mar 2026.

**QC.3 What would you actually do as a JE (Electrical) in PWD (Buildings)?**
A: No Meghalaya duty chart is published, but a PWD electrical JE generally:
- surveys sites and prepares preliminary and detailed estimates from the SOR;
- supervises contractors during execution;
- checks materials against approved makes and ISI marks;
- records measurements in the MB and prepares running and final bills;
- tests installations (insulation, earth, polarity) and coordinates with MePDCL and the Electrical Inspector for connection and approval;
- maintains existing installations in government buildings, such as lifts, DG sets, pumps and substations;
- keeps site records: the site order book, material at site and hindrance register.
Source: general CPWD/PWD practice (CPWD Works Manual 2022). No Meghalaya-specific duty list was found.

**QC.4 Walk us through the electrification of a new government building, from drawings to handover.**
A:
1. Study the architectural drawings and prepare the electrical layout, load calculation and estimate (AA&ES and technical sanction come first).
2. After tendering, lay conduits and junction boxes in the slab before casting. This is the step that cannot be redone, so check it before every pour.
3. Approve material samples and check makes.
4. Do the wiring, DBs, sub-mains and the main LT panel, then the earth pits and lightning protection.
5. Test: insulation resistance, earth resistance, polarity and continuity, with RCD trip tests.
6. Apply for the supply connection and get inspection or self-certification as required under CEA 2023.
7. Prepare as-built drawings, take measurements and pay the final bill.
8. Hand over with the test reports. The defect liability period then starts.

**QC.5 How do you check material delivered to site?**
A:
- Compare it with the approved make list and the specification in the agreement.
- Check the ISI/BIS mark and the manufacturer's test certificates.
- Match it with the approved sample.
- For cables, check size, cores, conductor material, voltage grade and the length printed on the sheath.
- Enter it in the material-at-site register.
- Reject and remove anything not conforming, and record it in writing.

**QC.6 What lesson do you take from the 2022 dome collapse at the new Assembly building?**
A: On 22 May 2022 the dome of the under-construction Assembly building at New Shillong collapsed, fortunately with no casualties. The IIT Guwahati probe pointed to the dome's heavy dead weight and poor coordination between agencies, and it was redesigned as a lighter steel structure. The lesson for any PWD engineer, electrical included, is that design checks, coordination between agencies and site supervision cannot be skipped. A JE's signature in the MB and the site record is a safety responsibility, not a formality.
Source: EastMojo, 22 May 2022 and 10 Sep 2022 (secondary).
