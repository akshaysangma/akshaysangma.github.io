| Fact | Detail | Status / source |

### 1.9 Tariff (MSERC)

- **Regulator:** the Meghalaya State Electricity Regulatory Commission (MSERC), under the Electricity Act 2003. It sets tariffs through multi-year tariff (MYT) orders.
  - **MSERC MYT Regulations 2026** were notified in Aug 2026. UNVERIFIED (snippet): https://solarquarter.com/2026/08/10/mserc-notifies-myt-regulations-2026-for-meghalayas-power-sector/
  - A **Supply Code Regulations 2026** and a State Grid Code 2026 are also in process.

**FY 2025-26 order** (Case 09/2024, dated 24.03.2025). VERIFIED.
- **Domestic tariffs were CUT**:
  - First 100 units: ₹5.55 → **₹5.00**
  - Next 100 units: ₹5.65 → **₹5.04**
  - Above 200 units: ₹6.12 → **₹5.10**
  - Fixed charge unchanged at ₹90/kVA/month.
- MePDCL had asked for a "uniform tariff hike across all categories" to cover the revenue gap. MSERC instead rationalised cross-subsidy.
- Approved revenue gap at existing tariffs: ₹339.80 cr.

## 6. Likely interview questions with model answers (built from the verified facts above)

1. **Q: Tell us about your organisation.**
   A: I work as a contractual JE (Electrical) with MeECL [say which company you are under, e.g. MePDCL, the distribution subsidiary]. MeECL was incorporated on 14 Sep 2009 as the holding company when the Meghalaya State Electricity Board, set up on 21 Jan 1975, was unbundled under the Power Sector Reforms Transfer Scheme 2010. Its subsidiaries are MePGCL for generation, MePTCL for transmission and MePDCL for distribution. Assets moved to them from 1 Apr 2012. The CMD is Shri Sanjay Goyal, IAS, who is also Commissioner and Secretary of the Power Department.

2. **Q: Who is the Power Minister of Meghalaya?**
   A: Shri Metbah Lyngdoh of the UDP, MLA from Mairang. He took the Power portfolio in the September 2025 reshuffle, taking over from Shri A.T. Mondal.
   - (Re-check on interview day; a reshuffle is pending.)

3. **Q: What is Meghalaya's installed generating capacity?**
   A: MePGCL has about 368 MW, all hydro; it is 378.77 MW if the old 11.2 MW Umtru station is counted. Meghalaya has no thermal plant of its own. The state also has about 377 MW allocated from central stations of NEEPCO, NHPC and NTPC, and from OTPC.

4. **Q: Name the state's hydro stations.**
   A: Umiam Stage-I (36 MW, 1965), Stage-II (20 MW), Stage-III at Kyrdemkulai (60 MW), Stage-IV at Nongkhyllem (60 MW), the old Umtru station (11.2 MW), Myntdu-Leshka (126 MW, the largest), New Umtru (40 MW, 2017), Ganol (22.5 MW, 2023), and the Sonapani and Lakroh mini hydels of 1.5 MW each.

5. **Q: Which was the first power project in Meghalaya?**
   A: The Sonapani mini hydel in Shillong, commissioned in 1922 by a company owned by Dr B.C. Roy. Umtru, commissioned in 1957, was the North-East's first small hydro project. Umiam Stage-I in 1965 was the region's first reservoir project.

6. **Q: You are from Tura. Tell us about the Ganol project.**
   A: It is a 22.5 MW run-of-river project of three 7.5 MW units on the Ganol river near Tura, and the only state hydro station in Garo Hills. It cost about ₹177.5 crore. Work began in 2014 for completion in 2017. The CM inaugurated the first unit on 4 January 2023, and MSERC records commercial operation of the full plant from 1 August 2023. Its design energy is 67 MU. The inquiry report cited it for time and cost overruns.

7. **Q: What is Meghalaya's peak demand?**
   A: About 404 MW in 2023-24, according to the Power Policy 2024; peak demand has generally been 350–400 MW. In 2026 the CMD said average demand is around 180–200 MW. The state has surplus power in the monsoon and buys from the exchanges and NTPC Bongaigaon in the lean season.

8. **Q: Why does Meghalaya still face shortages despite its hydro potential?**
   A: The state's hydro potential is about 3,000 MW, but only about 13% has been harnessed, and all the generation depends on rainfall. The base load is about 250 MW, while firm base supply is only about 140 MW. Large projects such as NEEPCO's 85 MW Wah Umiam Stage-III and the 800 MW Wah Umiam project are stalled because land cannot be acquired under Sixth Schedule community ownership.

9. **Q: What are MePDCL's AT&C losses?**
   A: Brickwork's September 2026 rating note puts them at 21.28% in FY25 and 13.51% provisionally in FY26, with collection efficiency reaching 100%. The Minister told the Assembly 10.76% for FY25. The national figure is 15.04% for FY25. In 2018-19 the losses were above 37%.

10. **Q: Describe MeECL's financial position.**
    A: It was in a debt trap: losses of over ₹1,800 crore up to 2018 and overdue loan instalments. In 2021 the state backed a ₹1,345 crore REC loan to clear dues to generators. The consolidated company made its first profit, ₹162.68 crore, in FY26, but its net worth is still about −₹3,660 crore. Its ₹630 crore of bonds are rated A- (CE) only because of the state guarantee; without the guarantee the rating is B+.

11. **Q: What is RDSS and how is it implemented in Meghalaya?**
    A: The Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme has two parts, loss-reduction works and prepaid smart metering, and aims to cut AT&C losses to 12–15%. In Meghalaya, M/s Anvil Cables of Kolkata is the smart-metering agency, approved in March 2025 after a re-tender. As of 8 February 2026, 1,22,593 smart meters had been installed. MePDCL has also opened a transformer testing lab at Sumer to check transformers bought under RDSS.

12. **Q: There are complaints about smart meters. How would you handle them?**
    A: First, test the meter against a check meter. The Consumer Rights Rules require a check meter within 5 days of a dispute. Replace faulty meters free of cost and explain the bill to the consumer. Complaints can go to the 1912 helpline or CM Connect on 1971. The Union Power Minister has said prepaid metering is not compulsory for consumers.

13. **Q: What is the domestic tariff in Meghalaya?**
    A: For FY 2026-27, MSERC's order of 26 March 2026 sets ₹5.00, ₹5.04 and ₹5.10 per unit for the 0–100, 101–200 and above-200 unit slabs, plus a fixed charge of ₹90 per kVA per month. Kutir Jyoti consumers pay ₹4.57 per unit up to 30 units. Domestic rates were actually cut in FY 2025-26, from ₹5.55, ₹5.65 and ₹6.12.

14. **Q: Who regulates tariffs?**
    A: The Meghalaya State Electricity Regulatory Commission, under the Electricity Act 2003. It issues multi-year tariff orders after public hearings. Its Chairman is Shri Chandan Kumar Mondol.

15. **Q: What is the distribution franchisee issue?**
    A: MePDCL has an input-based franchisee, SAI Computers, in some areas such as Dalu. MePDCL keeps the 33 kV network, and the franchisee runs the 11 kV network and below, plus billing. After complaints, the CM held a review in June 2026 and directed that service and meter replacement come before billing, with no sudden burden on consumers. It is a model for cutting losses, but consumers have to be won over first.

16. **Q: How is Tura supplied with power?**
    A: Mainly from the 132 kV Rongkhon substation near Tura, plus Ganol's output. The bigger change is the Bongaigaon–Nangalbibra line, built for 400 kV and run at 220 kV, with a 220/132 kV, 2 × 160 MVA substation at Nangalbibra commissioned in February 2025. In MePDCL, the Tura Circle has the Tura Division with the Tura East, North and West sub-divisions.

17. **Q: What did the Meghalaya Power Policy 2024 propose?**
    A: Development of hydro through CPSUs and joint ventures, a framework for pumped storage at existing reservoirs, at least 100 MW of state solar by 2030, wind development, and support for EVs. It sets RPO targets rising from 29.15% in FY25 to 41.08% in FY30, and plans a first thermal plant to cover the base-load gap.

18. **Q: What is the CM Solar Mission?**
    A: It was launched on 11 August 2023 with ₹500 crore over five years. It gives 50–70% subsidy for solar units, with bank linkage, and aims for 200–300 MW of solar. It complements PM Surya Ghar, the scheme of 13 February 2024 for 1 crore households, which had reached over 50 lakh households by August 2026.

19. **Q: Who are the Governor, CM, Deputy CMs and Chief Secretary?**
    A: Governor C.H. Vijayashankar. Chief Minister Conrad K. Sangma. Deputy CMs Prestone Tynsong, who also holds PWD Roads and Buildings, and Sniawbhalang Dhar. Chief Secretary Dr Shakil P. Ahammed, in post since 30 September 2025.

20. **Q: Who is the Union Power Minister, and what is India's installed capacity?**
    A: Shri Manohar Lal; the MoS is Shripad Naik. Capacity is about 560 GW as of September–October 2026, of which non-fossil sources are about 55% (around 308 GW). Non-fossil capacity crossed 50% on 30 June 2025, five years ahead of the 2030 NDC target. The goal is 500 GW of non-fossil capacity by 2030 and net zero by 2070.

21. **Q: What was the all-time peak demand?**
    A: About 271 GW, met in May 2026.

22. **Q: Have you heard of the Khavda grid event?**
    A: Yes. On 13 May 2026 a fault led to the loss of about 9 GW of renewable generation at the Khavda complex in Gujarat. Frequency fell to about 49.4 Hz and under-frequency relays shed about 6 GW of load. It shows why grid-forming capability and storage matter as renewable share grows.

23. **Q: What is the SHANTI Act?**
    A: It received assent on 20 December 2025 and replaces the Atomic Energy Act 1962 and the CLND Act 2010. It allows licensed private operators in nuclear power, caps operator liability, creates a liability fund and gives the AERB statutory status. It supports the target of 100 GW of nuclear power by 2047.

24. **Q: What is the status of the Electricity (Amendment) Bill?**
    A: The draft came out on 9 October 2025. It proposes cost-reflective tariffs, phasing out cross-subsidy for industry and railways, and multiple suppliers on one network. As of now it remains a draft and has not been passed.

25. **Q: As a PWD electrical engineer, what current state projects interest you?**
    A: Several:
    - The new Assembly building at New Shillong: the dome collapsed in 2022 and was redesigned with IIT Guwahati and IIT Roorkee; the Speaker expects completion by the end of 2026.
    - Venues for the 39th National Games in 2027 in Shillong, Tura and Jowai, including the P.A. Sangma complex in Tura.
    - Tura Medical College, where the 80-bed block is due in December 2026.
    - Building electrification with rooftop solar under the CM Solar Mission.
