JOINs deep dive

Most interview SQL is a JOIN. Get the four shapes, the LEFT-JOIN-NULL pattern, and the row-multiplication trap, and you're most of the way there.

~13 min Chapter 1 · Foundations Highest-value lesson Retrieval practice inside
The win You'll pick the right join for "matching rows", "keep everything on one side", and "rows with no match", and you'll explain fan-out — why a careless join can multiply your row counts and wreck a SUM.

A join combines rows from two tables on a condition

Conceptually (logical step 1, FROM): take every pairing of rows across the two tables, then keep the pairings where the ON condition is true. The join type decides what happens to rows that found no match.

The four shapes. A = left table, B = right table.
JoinKeepsUse when
INNER JOINOnly rows matched in bothYou want records that exist on both sides
LEFT JOINAll of A; B columns NULL when unmatched"Every customer, with their orders if any"
RIGHT JOINAll of B; mirror of LEFTRare — usually rewrite as a LEFT join
CROSS JOINEvery pairing (Cartesian product)Deliberate combinations (e.g. all sizes × colours)

The anti-join: "rows in A with no match in B"

A hugely common interview ask — "customers who never ordered." The idiom is LEFT JOIN then keep the NULLs:

SELECT   c.id, c.name
FROM     customers c
LEFT JOIN orders o ON o.customer_id = c.id
WHERE    o.id IS NULL;          -- no matching order existed
Why it works The LEFT JOIN keeps every customer; unmatched ones get all-NULL order columns. Filtering o.id IS NULL (a column that's NOT NULL when a match exists) keeps exactly the customers with zero orders.

The trap: ON vs WHERE on a LEFT JOIN

Trap — filtering the outer side Putting a condition on the right table in WHERE silently turns a LEFT JOIN back into an INNER JOIN: WHERE o.status='paid' drops the NULL rows you were trying to keep. If the condition must preserve unmatched rows, put it in the ON clause instead: LEFT JOIN orders o ON o.customer_id=c.id AND o.status='paid'.

The trap: fan-out (row multiplication)

Trap — joins multiply rows Joining orders to order_items (one-to-many) produces one row per item, not per order. Now SUM(orders.total) double-counts — once per item. Aggregate to the right grain first (or SUM the item-level column), and sanity-check row counts before and after a join.

Check yourself

"List every product, including ones never sold." Which join, from products to sales?
B. "Every product, sold or not" means keep all of the left table (products) even with no matching sale — that's a LEFT JOIN. INNER would drop unsold products; CROSS pairs everything; RIGHT would keep all sales, the wrong side.
A LEFT JOIN with WHERE b.status = 'x' returns no unmatched rows. Why?
C. Unmatched left rows have NULL in every right column, so b.status = 'x' is UNKNOWN for them and WHERE drops them — collapsing the LEFT JOIN to an INNER. Move that condition into the ON clause to keep them.
