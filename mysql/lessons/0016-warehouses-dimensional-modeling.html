Warehouses & dimensional modeling

How the analytics destination is shaped. The star schema is the most-asked data-modeling pattern in interviews — facts in the middle, dimensions around them.

~12 min Chapter 3 · Data pipelines Interview favourite Retrieval practice inside
The win You'll draw a star schema, tell a fact table from a dimension, define grain, and explain why this deliberate denormalization is the right call for analytics — the mirror image of Lesson 7.

Facts and dimensions

Kimball's dimensional model splits analytics data into two kinds of table:

The two table types in a star schema.
Fact tableDimension table
HoldsMeasurable numeric events (sale amount, quantity)Descriptive context (customer, product, date)
RowsMany — one per business eventFewer — one per entity
KeysForeign keys to every dimension + the measuresA surrogate key + descriptive attributes

Picture fact_sales in the center, with foreign keys radiating out to dim_customer, dim_product, dim_date, dim_store — that radial shape is the star schema. A query joins the fact to a few dimensions and aggregates: "sum amount by dim_date.month and dim_store.region."

Grain — decide it first The grain is what one fact row represents ("one line item per order"). Kimball's rule: choose the most detailed atomic grain the process records. Fine grain can always be aggregated up; a pre-aggregated fact table can never be drilled back down.
Star vs snowflake A star keeps dimensions denormalized (flat, wide) for fast, simple joins. A snowflake re-normalizes dimensions into sub-tables — tidier, but more joins and slower reads. For analytics, prefer the star unless a dimension is genuinely huge. This is exactly the "denormalize for reads" trade-off from Lesson 7.

Check yourself

In a star schema, what does the fact table hold?
A. The fact table stores the measurable events (the numbers you aggregate) plus foreign keys out to the dimensions. The descriptive attributes (B) live in the dimension tables — that division is the whole point of the model.
Kimball says choose the fact table's grain at what level?
A. Pick the finest atomic grain — you can always roll detailed rows up, but you can never split pre-aggregated rows back down. Modeling to one dashboard's summary (C) paints you into a corner.
